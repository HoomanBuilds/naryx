# services/api

Private backend-for-frontend for the operator terminal.

The service exposes live package snapshots and previews plus injected boundaries for preparing unsigned Solana Devnet transactions and observing a submitted Devnet signature. The default process has no execution ports, so preparation and observation fail with `EXECUTION_UNAVAILABLE`.

`GET /internal/terminal/snapshot?domain=` and `POST /internal/terminal/preview` serve no fixture numbers. Each domain is backed by a `TerminalMarketSource` (`src/private-terminal-manifest.ts`): a static `descriptor` (environment, package id, base and quote symbols and decimals, maximum size atoms, maximum slippage bps, `maxStalenessMs`, settlement label, and the spot and perpetual leg instrument and venue) and `latest()`, which returns the newest unsigned observation `{ spotBid, spotAsk, perpBid, perpAsk, spotTakerRate, perpTakerRate, capturedAtMs }` as decimal strings in quote units per base unit. `main.ts` composes one source per configured lane: Hyperliquid from `HyperliquidTestnetPriceFeed` (staleness `orderContext.maxStalenessMs`), Base from the Uniswap V3 pool and the Chainlink-priced test perpetual, Arbitrum from the Uniswap V3 pool and the GMX market's Chainlink-compatible feed, and Solana from the Pyth-priced test perpetual and the solver's firm inventory. An AMM or oracle leg quotes one marginal price for both sides, with its fee as the taker rate. A domain whose runtime is not configured answers `503 DOMAIN_MARKET_UNAVAILABLE`. An observation that is missing, older than `maxStalenessMs`, newer than the local clock, crossed, or malformed answers `503 SNAPSHOT_UNAVAILABLE` or `503 PREVIEW_UNAVAILABLE`. Live responses carry `source` `PRIVATE_TERMINAL_BFF`, the descriptor's `environment`, `evidenceGrade` `OBSERVED_UNATTESTED` (observed, unsigned, not attested), and an ISO `capturedAt` equal to the observation's local receipt time. Snapshot references are the exact spot and perpetual mids, basis is `(perp mid - spot mid) / spot mid` in bps truncated toward zero to 0.01 bps, and a domain reads `Live` only while its source is fresh. The preview entry bound is the canonical order's `maxSpotQuoteIn`, `ceil(size * spot ask * (10000 + slippageBps) / 10000)` quote atoms; the exit bound is `floor(size * spot bid * (10000 - slippageBps) / 10000)`; leg limits are the exact touch price widened by the slippage; and each fee is the leg notional at the touch price times the observed taker rate, rounded up. Size precision, maximum size, and slippage are checked against the descriptor (`400 INVALID_SIZE` or `INVALID_SLIPPAGE`). `executionEnabled` and `executionAvailable` are true only when that domain's composed runtime is available and the execution readiness gate is configured, the same conditions `/internal/healthz` reports.

With `NARYX_REFERENCE_HISTORY_DB` set, `src/reference-history.ts` samples every composed domain's `TerminalMarketSource` every 15 seconds into a durable SQLite store and keeps 30 days. A sample is the observation's capture time, the exact spot and perpetual mids (a locked mid-only or oracle-priced book is accepted; a crossed one is not), the basis in hundredths of a bp truncated toward zero, and the named source of each leg (for example the Uniswap V3 pool, the Chainlink or Pyth account, or the Hyperliquid book). A missing, stale, or future-dated observation records nothing and a repeated observation is recorded once, so a gap stays a gap. `GET /internal/terminal/reference-candles?domain=&series=spot|perp|basis&interval=1m|5m|15m|1h|4h|1d&limit=` (limit 1 to 1000, default 300) answers the `limit` UTC buckets ending now as first, highest, lowest, and last sample candles with exact decimal strings and per-candle sample counts, omits empty buckets, and carries the window's source labels, the lane's base and quote symbols, the latest observation time, and the methodology text. Any other query key, a repeated key, or a value outside those sets answers `400 INVALID_QUERY`. Each lane, series, and interval is read once at the largest limit and clipped per request, cached 5 s, or 60 s for hourly and coarser intervals, so no query can force repeated scans. A store that cannot open logs the reason and turns the history off; trading is unaffected. No history is backfilled.

Preparation accepts only a normalized cash-and-carry request. The injected port owns route compilation and returns an unsigned materialization. The HTTP boundary independently verifies the Devnet identity, plan kind, trader signer, zero signatures, wire size, resolved-account count, compute-unit cap, lookup commitments, and evidence before returning `DEVNET_UNSIGNED_REVIEW_REQUIRED`.

Observation is read-only and reports `SUBMITTED`, `FINALIZED`, `FAILED`, or `EXPIRED`. The observation boundary has no signer, wallet authority, submission, simulation, broadcast, arbitrary instruction input, RPC URL input, or mainnet path.

Every terminal execution handoff is additionally protected by an injected Phase 8 readiness gate. The gate defaults to unavailable even when a venue runtime is configured. It recomputes the canonical readiness decision, requires `READY`, and requires one exact funded-operation manifest binding for environment, domain manifest, template manifest, settlement class, quote mode, size cohort, asset, operation and account commitments, action, principal, network fee, protocol fee, slippage, margin, recovery, loss, validity, runtime and configuration hashes, authority inventory, and complete categorized readiness evidence records. Canonical readiness evaluation also enforces aggregate asset and account budgets. The gate persists the exact funded-operation and readiness-decision hashes before releasing the handoff. Mainnet domains and environments are rejected unconditionally. Discovery, quoting, lifecycle reading, and other read-only evidence paths do not gain signing or write authority from this gate.

The process composes `TestnetCapExecutionGate` when `NARYX_EXECUTION_POLICY_FILE` and `NARYX_EXECUTION_POLICY_DB` are both set (absolute paths outside the repository). It approves public-testnet handoffs automatically within operator caps: `DurableAttemptScopeResolver` resolves every handoff to the durable canonical order behind its selected attempt (Solana Devnet preparation by the order's idempotency key), and the order's own worst-case quote spend (spot quote cap plus margin cap) is checked against the domain's per-operation and per-UTC-day caps, together with its signed recovery-loss bound. Only domains listed in the policy are accepted, the order's quote asset must be the asset the caps are set in, and mainnet domains and environments are refused regardless of the file. An attempt is approved once and counted once; its later handoffs reuse the approval, and a rebind to a different order is refused. Every allow and deny is recorded in the decision database before the handoff proceeds. The policy file is re-read on each decision; an unreadable file makes execution unavailable rather than approved. The gate cannot run alongside the local fixture mode.

`FileExecutionReadinessPolicyProvider` consumes the canonical protocol-types `ReadinessDecisionInput` directly from an absolute external protocol-JSON file. It does not define another manifest format and reloads and validates the decision for every authorization. A trusted server-side scope resolver must independently derive the actual operation from durable attempt evidence. The browser cannot provide the scope. Durable gate evidence uses an external SQLite path. Until the policy provider, scope resolver, gate, and evidence store are all explicitly composed, local, Devnet, Base Sepolia, Arbitrum Sepolia, and Hyperliquid Testnet handoffs remain unavailable.

The service also exposes an internal unsigned-order boundary at POST `/internal/terminal/orders` and GET `/internal/terminal/orders/{orderHash}`. The browser request carries only `contextId`, `owner`, `settlementAccount`, `size`, `slippageBps`, and `idempotencyKey`; size is parsed to integer atoms with the server-owned active context base-asset decimals and the authoritative clock is injected by the server. Orders are stored as `UNSIGNED_CREATED` and responses state that trader authorization and solver quoting are still required. The default process has no order ports, so both routes fail with `ORDER_CREATION_UNAVAILABLE`. This boundary performs no signing, quote creation, transaction preparation, submission, simulation, broadcast, or mainnet path.

It binds to loopback by default. Cross-origin browser access is allowed only for the exact configured terminal origin.

`.env.example` separates real testnet configuration from local development settings. The order, lifecycle, and execution-intent stores (`NARYX_API_ORDER_DB`, `NARYX_API_LIFECYCLE_DB`, `NARYX_API_EXECUTION_INTENT_DB`) are required absolute paths outside the checkout: startup fails and names the missing variable instead of defaulting to a temporary directory. `NARYX_SOLVER_INTERNAL_ORIGIN` names the loopback solver used for order quotes and local execution authorization.

No local order context is registered by default. `NARYX_LOCAL_FIXTURE_MODE=true` is the only way to compose the fixed-price local catalog and the fixture lifecycle routes (`POST /internal/terminal/attempts/{attemptId}/prepare`, `open`, `observation-ambiguity`, `controller-recovery`, and `close`), whose receipts are recorded by the service rather than observed. It requires a loopback `NARYX_API_HOST`, lets unset store paths default to `/tmp/naryx-local`, and refuses to start together with `NARYX_SOLANA_LOCAL_ENVIRONMENT_MANIFEST` or any testnet runtime flag, so fixture receipts never share a database with testnet evidence. Without it the fixture routes answer `409 FIXTURE_MODE_DISABLED`. `NARYX_SOLANA_LOCAL_ENVIRONMENT_MANIFEST` instead composes the manifest-validated local Solana validator and requires `NARYX_API_SOLANA_LOCAL_PREPARATION_DB`.

An enabled runtime that fails to compose stays unavailable and writes one stderr line with the runtime name and the first line of the error message, with URLs and credential parameters redacted. Manifests, keys, and request bodies are never logged.

`GET /internal/healthz` reports what was composed, always with HTTP 200. `environment` is `TESTNET` when any public testnet runtime (Solana Devnet, Base Sepolia, Arbitrum Sepolia, Hyperliquid Testnet) is enabled, `LOCAL_CONFORMANCE` only in fixture mode, `LOCAL_VALIDATOR` for the manifest-validated local runtime, and `UNCONFIGURED` otherwise. `status` is `degraded` when an enabled runtime is unavailable, `unconfigured` when nothing is composed, and `ready` otherwise. `localAtomicRuntimeMode` is `PHASE4_FIXTURE`, `MANIFEST_VALIDATED`, or `DISABLED`, and `runtime` carries each boundary's availability and reason.

Concrete Devnet ports are built by `createSolanaDevnetExecutionPorts`. The factory must receive reviewed server-owned contexts and a trusted Devnet RPC before the existing server can use it.

The Solana Devnet runtime is disabled by default. Enabling it requires an absolute `NARYX_SOLANA_DEVNET_RUNTIME_MANIFEST` path to an external immutable protocol-JSON manifest in `ACTIVE` state, a signerless `NARYX_SOLANA_DEVNET_RPC_URL`, a durable preparation database outside the checkout, and a loopback read-only binding source. Startup verifies the Devnet genesis and the exact core, reservation, package-book, perpetual-adapter, and perpetual-venue program and ProgramData identities. Each preparation resolves only a durable selected attempt, canonical order, and signed quote, then validates domain, registry, resource, series, reservation, lookup-table, compute-unit, address-count, packet-size, and postcondition bindings before returning an unsigned zero-signature transaction.

The loopback binding source must materialize current finalized account evidence. The API validates that evidence against the reviewed deployment and the adapter compiler, but it does not submit, simulate, sign, airdrop, deploy, initialize, or independently attest the upstream source. The browser remains the only eventual Devnet transaction sender after human review. A stale or mismatched binding, wrong genesis, changed program identity, missing durable evidence, or unsupported configuration fails closed. There is no mainnet or default-wallet fallback.

The Base Sepolia atomic boundary is disabled by default. When enabled, `NARYX_BASE_SEPOLIA_RUNTIME_MANIFEST` must name an external immutable protocol-JSON manifest containing the exact reviewed deployment, domain, admission, execution-bound, finality, and `ACTIVE` activation identities. `NARYX_BASE_SEPOLIA_RPC_URL` supplies a signerless read endpoint. Startup verifies chain ID and every deployment bytecode hash, and each attempt rechecks them before materializing durable selected order and quote evidence. Missing, inactive, stale, or mismatched identity fails the boundary closed. The API has no wallet, signing, submission, or broadcast path. Prepared attempts and the transaction hash bound by observation live in the SQLite database at `NARYX_BASE_SEPOLIA_PREPARATION_DB`, required when the runtime is enabled, so replay and observation survive a restart. Each row is revalidated against its request commitment on read, an idempotency key reused with a different attempt, trader signature, or preparation fails closed, and a transaction hash binds once. Beside each bound transaction the same database keeps what the latest observation proved about it: `PENDING` (no settled receipt yet), `REVERTED` or `FINALIZED` with the block number and package receipt hash from the receipt, or `EXPIRED` when no receipt exists while the latest block's timestamp is at or past the signed package deadline, after which the package verifier reverts any inclusion. An evidence mismatch records nothing, a receipt replaces `EXPIRED`, and `REVERTED` and `FINALIZED` never change. Each minute the API observes up to ten bound transactions without a settled outcome again, so an outcome lands after the sending browser is gone.

The Arbitrum Sepolia asynchronous boundary is also disabled by default. When enabled, `NARYX_ARBITRUM_SEPOLIA_RUNTIME_MANIFEST` must name an explicit absolute external path to an immutable protocol-JSON manifest with `ACTIVE` state, the reviewed domain and deployment identities, exact GMX dependencies, route and series constraints, finality and evidence requirements, and the deployment observation start block. `NARYX_ARBITRUM_SEPOLIA_RPC_URL` supplies a signerless viem public client. Startup and every observation verify chain ID, deployed bytecode hashes, coordinator admission, isolated-account controller, adapter account binding, and execution-class identity. Attempt evidence is discovered from coordinator transition and request-registration logs at or after the immutable deployment block and accepted only when live coordinator terms match the durable order, quote, route, series, execution-class, and evidence-schema hashes exactly.

This runtime exposes only the existing asynchronous observation path. It can report coordinator, entry-adapter, recovery, conflict, and finalized exit evidence that already exists onchain. It cannot reserve a package, create a GMX request, fund execution, recover or exit a position, sign, submit, simulate, or broadcast. Missing logs, duplicate package matches, incomplete evidence, wrong-chain RPCs, altered code, and configuration mismatches fail closed. A selected Arbitrum attempt (`arbitrum-async-*`) is readable through `GET /internal/terminal/attempts/{attemptId}` and the loopback `GET /internal/solver/attempts/{attemptId}`, like local and Base attempts.

Arbitrum Sepolia entry orders are opt-in. With `NARYX_ARBITRUM_SEPOLIA_ORDER_CONTEXT_ENABLED=true`, `src/arbitrum-sepolia-order-context.ts` loads `NARYX_ARBITRUM_SEPOLIA_ORDER_CONTEXT` (absolute protocol JSON) and the deployment from `NARYX_ARBITRUM_SEPOLIA_RUNTIME_MANIFEST`, and reads through a signerless client on `NARYX_ARBITRUM_SEPOLIA_RPC_URL`. The fields are `schemaVersion: 1`, `contextId`, `orderVersion`, `templateId`, `templateVersion`, `packageTemplateManifestHash`, `baseAsset`, `quoteAsset`, `spotAdapter`, `perpetualAdapter`, `priceFeed` (`{ address, expectedCodeHash }`), `priceFeedDecimals`, `maxStalenessSeconds`, `pollIntervalMs`, `expiryTtlSeconds`, `maxEntrySpread`, `maximumQuantityAtoms`, `maxSlippageBps`, `maxVenueFeeAtomsByAsset` (canonical order, including a zero base-asset cap), `maxMarginAddedAtoms`, `maxProtocolFeeAtoms`, `maxSolverFeeAtoms`, and `maxPriorityFeeAtoms`. Startup requires `eth_chainId == 421614`. The feed is polled with `latestRoundData`, and the price feed code hash and decimals are checked on every read. `capturedAtClock` is the round's `updatedAt`, `maxStaleness` is `maxStalenessSeconds`, and the order clock is the latest block timestamp, so `POST /internal/terminal/orders` refuses a stale price (`STALE_CONTEXT`). A failed, incomplete, or stale read clears the snapshot, and the context then answers `UNKNOWN_CONTEXT`. The reference price is the feed answer as quote atoms per base atom, with the quote asset taken as the feed's USD unit at par. Orders are `ASYNC_BONDED_SOLVER`, `EVM_UNIX_SECONDS`, `FOK`, and capped at the smaller of `maximumQuantityAtoms` and the deployment bound. Any wallet can trade: each owner settles through its own isolated account at the factory's `accountOf(owner)`, which the solver quote and executor enforce.

With `NARYX_ARBITRUM_SEPOLIA_EXECUTOR_CLIENT_ENABLED=true` and the Arbitrum runtime composed, `POST /internal/terminal/evm-testnet/observe-async` keeps its `ARBITRUM_TESTNET_ASYNC_HANDOFF` readiness gate. After the gate approves, `src/arbitrum-sepolia-executor-client.ts` first calls the solver executor at loopback `NARYX_ARBITRUM_SEPOLIA_EXECUTOR_ORIGIN` (`POST /internal/solver/arbitrum-sepolia/execute`, which is idempotent per attempt) and then runs the unchanged signerless observation for status. An unreachable executor, an invalid response, or a `FAILED` attempt fails the handoff closed. The API still holds no key and signs nothing. Each handoff records the attempt's chain-proven state in the SQLite database at `NARYX_ARBITRUM_SEPOLIA_OUTCOME_DB`, required with the executor client: an entry's coordinator state, an exit's `EXIT_<status>` once the exit controller knows its request, or `CLOSED` with the final package receipt, plus the transaction the solver's journal last confirmed. Not found and evidence mismatch record nothing, and a settled outcome never changes. Each minute the API reads up to ten unsettled attempts again through the signerless observation alone, which never advances the solver.

`GET /internal/terminal/packages?owner=<wallet>` lists the wallet's packages from the durable order store. Each entry's `state` is the lifecycle head where the lane records one (Solana); for Base and Arbitrum it is the recorded outcome above, with `transactionHash`, `blockNumber`, and `receiptHash`. An attempt the API never observed on chain has a null state.

Arbitrum exit uses the same owner-route CORS. `GET /internal/terminal/arbitrum-sepolia/account` reports `openPackage` read from chain (`activePackageOf`, the GMX short, spot inventory, and any active exit request key), so a package entered elsewhere can still be exited. When the runtime manifest names `exitController` and `spotPort`, `POST /internal/terminal/arbitrum-sepolia/exit-order` (`{ owner, slippageBps, idempotencyKey }`) stores a canonical `EXIT` order for the whole open package: the minimum spot proceeds are the quantity at the smaller of pool and reference price less the pool fee and slippage, rounded down, and the minimum perpetual output is collateral plus PnL at the slippage-widened reference less the larger close fee, with slippage applied again as an allowance for accrued borrowing and funding fees. An underwater position, a partial size, an exit already in progress, or slippage above `maxSlippageBps` is refused. `POST /internal/terminal/arbitrum-sepolia/prepare-exit-authorization` and `authorize-exit` relay the solver's unsigned `ExitAuthorization` to the browser wallet and its signature back, after checking the domain, digest, owner, proceeds recipients, package, and order, quote, and route hashes. Observation binds an exit attempt to the entry package's coordinator terms and to the `ExitSubmitted` log whose authorization digest the solver journaled. The readiness principal of an exit is zero (no spot cap and a zero margin cap); the domain, asset, and recovery-loss checks still apply.

The service also exposes POST `/internal/terminal/hyperliquid-testnet/execute`. The browser request carries only `attemptId` and `idempotencyKey`. The server-owned injected port owns the Testnet attempt and returns a sanitized `hypercore:testnet` `TESTNET` result. The default process has no port, so the route fails with `EXECUTION_UNAVAILABLE`. The current limit is one identified attempt per request with no plan, account, signer, or venue payload from the browser.

Hyperliquid Testnet is disabled unless `NARYX_HYPERLIQUID_TESTNET_RUNTIME_ENABLED=true`. Evidence preparation and executor submission have additional independent gates, `NARYX_HYPERLIQUID_TESTNET_EVIDENCE_ENABLED` and `NARYX_HYPERLIQUID_TESTNET_EXECUTOR_CLIENT_ENABLED`, which also default to false. Readiness is available only when both are enabled and correctly composed. The environment must be exactly `TESTNET`. The external runtime config must be strict protocol JSON with version 1 and the exact `hypercore:testnet` domain manifest, solver, series, execution class, market, and evidence-bound identities. Its optional `orderContext` enables canonical Testnet cash-and-carry order creation and binds the owner and settlement account to the configured dedicated trading account. The context carries exact quantity, fee, margin, residual, recovery, and expiry bounds. Without `orderContext`, Hyperliquid order creation remains unavailable.

Order prices are live, not configured. When `orderContext` is present the API starts `HyperliquidTestnetPriceFeed`, which reads only the pinned `https://api.hyperliquid-testnet.xyz/info` endpoint with a bounded timeout, no credentials, no redirects, and bounded response size. It resolves the perpetual coin from `meta` at `market.perpetual.assetIndex` and the spot coin from the `spotMeta` universe entry at `market.spot.universeIndex`, whose token pair must equal `market.spot.tokenIndex` and `market.quoteTokenIndex`. Every `orderContext.livePricing.refreshIntervalMs` (2000 to 60000; each refresh costs 24 of the 1200 per minute info weight) it reads both `l2Book`s and `userFees` for the trading account. A snapshot is accepted only when every level is a positive decimal in strict price order, the best bid is below the best ask, the spread is at most `maxBookSpreadBps` of the bid, each book time is no newer than local receipt and no older than `maxBookAgeMs`, and both taker rates are in `[0, 0.01)`. A failed refresh keeps the previous snapshot, is reported once to stderr until a refresh succeeds, and never resets the snapshot's capture time, so `maxStalenessMs` (above the refresh interval, at most 120000) eventually rejects order creation with `STALE_CONTEXT`. Until the first valid snapshot the context is unknown, so order creation, quoting, and selection for it return context errors; startup does not wait for it.

Each order context derives exact reduced prices from the latest snapshot and stamps `capturedAtClock` with its local receipt time. The spot reference price is the best spot ask, which bounds `maxSpotQuoteIn`. The perpetual sell floor is the best perpetual bid times `(10000 - maxSlippageBps) / 10000`, so `maxSlippageBps` must be below 10000. The residual valuation price is the spot mid. Recovery bounds are the spot or perpetual mid widened by `recoveryBandBps`: buy caps at `(10000 + band) / 10000` and sell floors at `(10000 - band) / 10000`. The adapter, solver, and keeper format each bound onto the HyperCore price grid using its rounding tag and reject a wire price outside the exact bound, so sell floors carry `CEIL` and buy caps carry `FLOOR`. HyperCore charges the spot taker fee in the received base asset, so the BOUNDED_NET net spot shortfall is `ceil(size * (ceil(userSpotCrossRate * 10000) + feeShortfallSlackBps) / 10000) + maxNetSpotShortfallAtoms` and never exceeds the size. The terminal residual caps stay absolute and must cover that shortfall at the intended size, because the adapter rejects a net spot interval endpoint beyond them. The evidence and executor origins must be separate loopback HTTP origins. The execution journal must use an absolute durable path outside the repository.

The private solver handoff is GET `/internal/solver/hyperliquid-testnet/attempts/{attemptId}`. It is loopback-only and returns protocol JSON with exactly `{ "version": 1, "attempt": ... }`, where `attempt` is the canonical handoff produced from durable selected order and quote evidence. The executor client posts only `attemptId` and `idempotencyKey` to the separate solver executor process, applies a bounded timeout and response size, and validates the exact Testnet result before durable storage. The API never loads a Hyperliquid signer and has no direct exchange transport or network write authority. Its only Hyperliquid network access is the read-only Testnet info price feed.

`SqlitePackageExchangeStore` persists the package exchange. Economic strategy series, series execution classes, and package matching policies are stored as canonical bytes plus protocol JSON under their canonical hashes. A published identity and version can never be re-registered with different content, and an execution class is accepted only when its series and a matching policy written for the same class are already registered. Each execution class has one package book. Every submission, implied-liquidity admission, source-version observation, amendment, cancellation, and halt runs the protocol-types matcher inside one immediate SQLite transaction. Allocations are append-only and replay by taker order id. Consumed source reservations are append-only under a global primary key, so a reservation is single-use across every book, and source versions only move forward so stale implied liquidity cannot return. Loaded documents, book state, and allocations are revalidated against their canonical hashes and policy and fail closed as `CORRUPT_ROW`. `allocationTape` pages a book's allocations in recorded order after an opaque cursor.

`createPublicApiHandler` serves the public v1 API and returns false for any path it does not own, including `/v1/solver/`, so a host server can compose it. It holds no signer and persists nothing.

Query parameters are whitelisted, POST bodies are protocol JSON of at most 64 KiB, other methods are refused with 405, CORS allows any origin without credentials, and requests are rate limited per client window. `SqliteRegistryStore` holds immutable domain, market, package-template, and solver capability manifests; a solver manifest is admitted only with a valid Ed25519 operator signature over its manifest hash, an unchanged operator key, and a non-decreasing nonce.

`createSolverApiHandler` serves the authenticated solver API under `/v1/solver/`, enabled by `NARYX_SOLVER_API_DB` together with `NARYX_REGISTRY_DB`. Manifest registration (`PUT /v1/solver/capability-manifest`, `POST /v1/solver/register`) is authenticated by the operator signature inside the manifest. Every other request carries `X-Naryx-Solver`, `X-Naryx-Key`, `X-Naryx-Timestamp`, `X-Naryx-Nonce`, and `X-Naryx-Signature`: an Ed25519 signature by a registered, currently valid quote key over `solverRequestDigest`, which binds the method, path and query, body hash, solver, key, time, and a single-use nonce. Requests outside a 30-second skew, reused nonces, unknown or expired keys, and altered bodies are refused with 401.

Setting `NARYX_MAKER_SOLVER_ID` adds a read-only private terminal snapshot for that solver at `/internal/terminal/maker/operations`. It reports current signed quote shards, exact-state fill totals, inventory utilization, and capacity status. It exposes no signing key and provides no mutation path.

Public reads add `GET /v1/markets/{id}/quotes` (live shard levels with quote mode, settlement class, and reference; killed shards, stale heartbeats, and expired levels are excluded) and `GET /v1/solvers/{id}/capacity`.

`SqliteQualificationStore`, enabled by `NARYX_QUALIFICATION_DB` with `NARYX_QUALIFICATION_AUTHORITIES` (comma-separated `keyId:base58Ed25519Key` pairs) and `NARYX_QUALIFICATION_ACTIVATION_DELAY` (in the records' time unit), appends a qualification record only with a valid authority signature over its hash and only when the kernel accepts it as the next link of that object's chain: a monitor tightens at once, and anything that loosens qualification is a reviewed activation with two reviewers taking effect no earlier than the delay. No HTTP route appends; operators append through the store.

`SqlitePrivateDeliveryStore`, enabled by `NARYX_PRIVATE_DELIVERY_DB` together with the solver API, backs the private RFQ relay and the sealed-auction coordinator.

- `POST /v1/rfqs/private` accepts envelopes the taker already encrypted, plus their ciphertext and a `senderSignature`. The envelope's `senderKeyId` must be the sender's canonical base58 Ed25519 public key, and `senderSignature` must be that key's signature over the envelope hash; otherwise the entry is refused as `SENDER_UNAUTHENTICATED` before its nonce is even looked up, so nobody can spend or probe another sender's nonces. Each authenticated envelope passes `admitPrivateRfqEnvelope` against the recipient's registered manifest: pinned suite, recipient key and validity, expiry, per-sender nonce replay, and the SHA-256 of the received ciphertext. The relay holds no decryption key and stores ciphertext and canonical metadata only. The SDK implements `hpke-x25519-sha256-aes256gcm` for request and response encryption. A release must pin that exact id in `NARYX_RFQ_PINNED_SUITES`; with no pinned suite the relay answers 503 `PRIVATE_PATH_UNAVAILABLE` rather than degrading silently.
- Solvers pull addressed envelopes, each with its sender signature so the recipient can authenticate the sender itself, with `GET /v1/solver/private-rfqs`, acknowledge with `POST .../ack`, and respond with `POST .../response`; a response must bind the envelope, the recipient, the taker's response key, and the order through `verifyPrivateRfqResponse`. `GET /v1/rfqs/private/{envelopeHash}` reports acknowledgement and returns the encrypted response; being stored is never reported as being delivered.
- `POST /v1/auctions/sealed` creates an auction whose deadlines are hashed into its identity; every eligible solver must be registered. Solvers commit and reveal with `POST /v1/solver/auctions/{hash}/commit` and `/reveal` at server time. Events are appended only when the kernel accepts them against the replayed state, and state is always recomputed from the log. `GET /v1/auctions/sealed/{hash}` shows only the phase and commitment count before close, and the deterministic result with the full event log after close.

It may depend on `packages/protocol-types`, `packages/adapter-core`, `packages/adapters/*`, and `deployments`. It must not depend on `apps/web`, `packages/sdk`, or another service's internals.

Offchain quote shards settle through `createOffchainShardSettlement`, the controller gate for domains such as Hyperliquid that have no package contract to consume shard capacity. Before a shard-level fill executes it requires the solver's manifest to be live, re-verifies the shard signature under a quote key valid now (so revoking a key stops fills against every shard it signed), takes the settlement time from the server clock in the level's unit, and settles through the durable fill ledger in `SqliteSolverApiStore.settleShardFill`: the kernel settlement check runs with everything already filled against that exact signed state, so no level is filled beyond its size and no state beyond its inventory; the price is the committed reference plus the level offset, never a caller's number; and each fill is recorded once under its `shardFillCommitment`, replaying even after the shard moves on.

The Hyperliquid Testnet evidence runtime is separate from execution. It resolves an attempt only from the durable selected intent, canonical order, and signed quote stores, verifies the exact domain, solver, route, market metadata, bounds, and expiry, and can call only loopback solver preparation and reconciliation endpoints. The executor client is independently gated and owns no signer. Neither partial runtime is reported ready by itself.

`FIRM_BONDED` quotes carry both a funded reservation and the performance bond that pays if the solver fails to honor it. The solver API accepts one only after `verifyQuoteBond` confirms, on the bond's live state, that it belongs to the quoting solver, covers failure to honor a funded reservation, is unreleased, outlives the quote plus its dispute window, has unencumbered balance, and has a per-claim cap covering the taker's fees. `NARYX_BOND_READER_CONFIG` (absolute JSON: `rpcUrl`, `vault`, `solvers` by address, `assets` by address) reads that state from the vault with a read-only `eth_call`; without it bonded quotes are refused.

Keeper executor (`SqliteKeeperExecutor`, `NARYX_KEEPER_EXECUTOR_DB`, which requires `NARYX_STRATEGY_DB`; health authorities in `NARYX_HEALTH_AUTHORITIES` as `keyId:base58Key` pairs): `POST /v1/health-snapshots` takes a `StrategyHealthSnapshot` with its authority id and that authority's Ed25519 signature over the snapshot hash; `GET /v1/strategies/{id}/health` serves only the newest snapshot that describes the strategy's current state hash. The private server alone mounts the loopback keeper routes (`GET /internal/keeper/strategies/{id}/health`, `POST /internal/keeper/plan`, `POST /internal/keeper/execute`, `GET /internal/keeper/queue`), which refuse any non-loopback caller and any request that carries an `Origin`. The executor prices exit-class actions (scheduled exit, recovery, emergency risk reduction) as a full close with `packageCloseCostIndex` over the strategy's signed positions and a rebalance as a trim of the legs that carry the excess delta; it verifies the strategy owner's signature over the authorization bytes, re-runs the kernel keeper gate against its own health view and plan, and queues an authorized action exactly once for the domain runtime that executes it. It holds no signing key.

Coordination (`SqliteCoordinationStore`, `NARYX_COORDINATION_DB`, which requires a non-mainnet `NARYX_PUBLIC_ENVIRONMENT`): `GET /v1/coordinations/{planHash}` serves a `CROSS_DOMAIN_PREPOSITIONED` plan, every piece of per-domain evidence, and the kernel replay (`replayCrossDomainCoordination`) of phase, domain statuses, interim exposure, next actions, and violations at the server clock in the plan's unit, or at the latest evidence time for slot-timed plans (`timeSource`). `GET /v1/recovery/incidents/{id}` serves a `MANUAL_CONTROLLED_RECOVERY` incident with its events and the `replayManualRecovery` state. `POST /v1/recovery/approvals` takes a named approver's Ed25519 signature over `manualRecoveryApprovalHash`, dated no later than the server clock and no earlier than the last event; approver ids are base58 Ed25519 keys. Plans, per-domain evidence, incidents, executions, refused automation, and baseline evidence are written only through the private server's loopback routes (`POST /internal/coordination/plans`, `/internal/coordination/events`, `/internal/recovery/incidents`, `/internal/recovery/events`), which refuse any request with an `Origin`. Every record is append-only, and nothing here moves funds or signs anything.

Builder attribution (`SqliteBuilderStore`, `NARYX_BUILDER_DB`, which requires `NARYX_EVIDENCE_DB` and a non-mainnet `NARYX_PUBLIC_ENVIRONMENT`): `POST /v1/builders` registers a `BuilderManifest` signed by its own identity key, advanced only by a higher nonce under the same key; `POST /v1/builders/attributions` takes the order owner's Ed25519 signature over the attribution hash, fixed once per order; `GET /v1/builders/{id}`, `/attribution`, and `/revenue` serve the manifest and each attributed order's charged builder fees, payable only on a successful receipt that passes `checkBuilderFee` against the exact attributed manifest (validity at order acceptance, domain, template, owner cap, and manifest cap, in basis points of the package notional). A builder gains attribution and payment rights only.

Revenue reconciliation (`SqliteRevenueLedger`) is an internal back-office boundary with no public route and no transfer authority. A successful receipt creates separate protocol, solver, builder, rebate, and recovery-refund claims, while monthly minimums and partner shares are explicit claims. A claim is not treated as cash collection. Every onchain transfer, builder instruction, invoice payment, offchain payment, or refund settles one named claim, cannot exceed its remaining atoms, and is idempotent by settlement id. Claims and settlements are append-only, exact per asset manifest, and queryable as due, settled, and outstanding balances for either party. Partner shares name their parent protocol claim and cannot exceed it, which prevents a fee from being collected or allocated twice across channels.
